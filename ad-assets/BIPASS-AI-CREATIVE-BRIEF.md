## 02 · Why it exists

**AI writing doesn't get clocked because it's bad. It gets clocked because it's better than the
person handing it in.**

A sophomore who says "bro" out loud does not write "Moreover, it is imperative to consider the
multifaceted implications." Teachers notice the jump before any software does. Detectors,
meanwhile, are probabilistic, change constantly, and produce false positives — which hits ESL
students hardest, since careful non-native English already reads "too even."

So Bipass AI attacks the mismatch, not the software. It brings polished machine text back down to
a believable human level, in the voice of the person submitting it. That is the whole product
idea, and it is also the reason the brand can stay on the right side of a conversation that gets
other tools banned from ad platforms.

**Who built it.** A small independent student team. No VC funding, no offices. They built it for
themselves first. That origin is usable in creative — it is the honest version of "for students,
by students," and it explains the pricing (one-time passes, no subscription trap) better than any
value proposition line could.

## 03 · What it actually does

Three moves in one product. Competitors only do the first one — that gap is the campaign.

1. **Humanize** — rewrites robotic phrasing into natural rhythm: varied sentence length, plain
   words, real transitions instead of stock ones.
2. **Level Matching** — drops the writing to a believable level: Beginner, Student, Academic, or
   Custom. This is the part nobody else has.
3. **Auto Typer** — a Chrome extension types the reviewed text into Google Docs character by
   character: variable speed, thinking pauses, the occasional typo it catches and fixes.

**Sequence matters in creative.** Humanize → Level → Type is the product's actual order and its
most film-able idea. Most ads in this category stop at "paste, click, done." Ours has a third act.

## 05 · Who we're talking to

One buyer, four states of mind. The buyer and the user are the same person — a student spending
their own money, usually under deadline.

**The undergrad (core).** 18–22, US/UK/AU. Uses ChatGPT the way older students used Wikipedia,
and knows their essay reads "off." Wants it to sound like them again. The *Student* level is built
for exactly this person and is the default recommendation.

**The ESL writer (highest empathy).** Writes careful, even, textbook English — and gets
false-flagged for it. Levels let them write at the level they actually speak. This is the most
defensible, most sympathetic story the brand has, and it should not be a footnote in the work.

**The applicant (premium).** Personal statements, grad applications, thesis chapters. Wants their
voice kept and the stiff AI seams smoothed. Buys the Annual Pass. Talk to them in a lower, calmer
register than the TikTok work.

**The 2 a.m. panicker (volume).** Finals week, 2,000 words, one hour. Doesn't read features —
reads speed and price. Buys the $5.99 Day Pass on a phone. Every performance ad should be legible
to this person in under two seconds.

**Channel reality:** the audience lives on TikTok and Instagram Reels, and the brand's own social
approach is faceless demonstration content — no presenter, no talking head, the entertainment and
the product demo are the same clip.

### The name problem — read this before writing a single headline

**Bipass AI (B-I-P-A-S-S) is not BypassAI.** They are unaffiliated competitors with near-identical
spoken names, and BypassAI currently outranks us in search. Two consequences: spoken-word media
must spell the name or show it long enough to read, and no execution may imply any connection
between the two brands. Treat the near-miss as a reason for a strong, distinctive visual wordmark
rather than as a joke to lean on.

## 07 · Voice and vocabulary

The brand sounds like a competent friend one year ahead of you, not like software. Dry, specific,
unhyped, occasionally lowercase.

**Say this**
- "Sound human" / "reads like a real student wrote it"
- "At your level" — levels are the ownable idea
- "Typed. Not pasted."
- "It sounds like me"
- "No subscription. No card to cancel."
- Concrete numbers: $5.99, four levels, 1–5 samples
- Real student texture — finals week, discussion posts, 2 a.m.

**Never say this**
- "Cheat," "get away with it," "beat your teacher"
- "Guaranteed," "100%," "undetectable," "always passes"
- Any promise about a specific detector's score
- "Plagiarism" in any form — wrong product, wrong problem
- Corporate SaaS register: "leverage," "revolutionary," "seamless"
- Anything implying a link to BypassAI or Undetectable.ai
- Claims that the Auto Typer proves a student wrote something

**Tone reference:** the product's own user reviews are the target register — *"honestly saved me
during finals week, pasted my essay, put it on Student level and it just read like me."* Lowercase,
specific, no exclamation marks. Ad copy that sounds more polished than that sounds less true.

## 10 · Creative territories

Six directions the product actually supports. Not exclusive — but a campaign that picks one and
commits will beat one that samples all six.

**01 · The Tell.** The gap between how a student talks and how their essay reads. Cut between the
two: someone saying "yeah it was kinda mid" over a screen reading "the exhibition proved somewhat
underwhelming." Bipass closes the gap. Highest-concept, most awardable route, and the one that
dramatises the insight instead of the interface.

**02 · Four Levels.** One paragraph, run four ways — Beginner, Student, Academic, Custom — as a
typographic set piece. Poster, carousel, and 6-second bumper all fall out of the same idea. Owns
the single feature no competitor can match, and works with zero voiceover.

**03 · Typed. Not Pasted.** Extreme macro on the caret. Characters appearing one at a time, a
pause, a typo, a backspace, the correction. Forty minutes compressed into eight seconds. The
brand's signature image, and nobody else in the category can run it.

**04 · Sounds Like Me.** The style profile as a fingerprint: your comma habits, your missing
apostrophes, the way you always start sentences with "So." The emotional route — for ESL students
and applicants especially — and the one that argues the tool restores a voice rather than
replacing one.

**05 · Nothing To Cancel.** Straight price attack on a category built on subscription traps. $5.99
once, credits that never expire, a free pass to start. Best for performance and paid social where
the two-second read is all you get.

**06 · 2 A.M.** Situational truth. Finals week, a blinking cursor, a word count that won't move.
The product arrives as relief, not as software. Cheap to produce, endlessly extendable, and the
natural home for relatable-pain posts — as long as the joke is about the deadline, never about
deceiving anyone.

## 11 · Claim guardrails

Non-negotiable. These protect ad-account health as much as legal exposure — the cheat framing is
what gets accounts in this category shadow-banned and rejected.

1. **Never promise a detector result.** No score, no pass rate, no "undetectable." The product's
   own copy says a detector score is an estimate and no tool can guarantee one. Creative may say
   text *reads* human; it may not say what any software will conclude.
2. **The Auto Typer types — that's all.** It is a typing convenience. It does not recreate a
   research or drafting process and does not prove authorship. Never suggest it manufactures
   evidence.
3. **Third-party detector brands.** Turnitin, GPTZero, Copyleaks and the rest may be referenced
   factually in editorial and organic demos, never in a way that implies partnership, and never
   with a fabricated score presented as a real result. Keep their logos and interfaces out of paid
   work.
4. **Integrity stays with the user.** The standing line: Bipass AI is a writing tool and how you
   use it is your responsibility, under your school's policy. Keep that visible in long-form work
   rather than buried in a legal end-card.
5. **Review is part of the product story.** Every honest demo ends with the student reading the
   output, not with a submit button. Facts, citations and meaning are theirs to check.
6. **Name and independence.** Spell B-I-P-A-S-S in any spoken execution. Never imply affiliation
   with BypassAI or similarly named sites — the site carries an explicit disclaimer and creative
   must not undercut it.
